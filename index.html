Topic: Linear Motion

Sentence Skeleton (Fill in the blanks to start!)

"Linear motion occurs when a body moves in a straight or curved line, with all its parts moving the same ___________, in the same ___________, over the same ___________."

Key Words & Definitions

  • Linear Motion: Movement of a body in a straight or curved line, where all parts move the same distance, in the same direction, over the same time.
  • Direct Force: A force applied straight through the Centre of Mass of an object. This is what *creates* linear motion.
  • Centre of Mass (CoM): The point at which the body is balanced in all directions.
  • Scalar Quantity: A measurement that has size (magnitude) only. (e.g., Distance, Speed).
  • Vector Quantity: A measurement that has both size (magnitude) AND direction. (e.g., Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration).

Creating Linear Motion

To generate purely linear motion without any spin (angular motion), a direct force must be applied exactly through the object's Centre of Mass.

Sporting Example: A football player taking a penalty kick who wants the ball to travel completely straight with no spin must strike the ball exactly through its centre of mass (a "knuckleball" strike).

Quantities of Linear Motion

Quantity Type Definition & Sporting Example Equation & Units
Distance Scalar The total length of the path travelled.
Example: A 400m runner covers a distance of 400m over one lap.		 Unit: metres (m)
Displacement Vector The shortest straight line distance from the start to the finish point, including direction.
Example: A 400m runner has a displacement of 0m when they finish, as they end up exactly where they started!		 Unit: metres (m)
Speed Scalar The rate of change of distance.
Example: A marathon runner running at a steady pace of 5 m/s.		 Speed = Distance / Time
Unit: m/s
Velocity Vector The rate of change of displacement.
Example: A 100m sprinter travelling at 10 m/s straight down the track.		 Velocity = Displacement / Time
Unit: m/s
Acceleration Vector The rate of change of velocity.
Example: A hockey player speeding up from a jog to a sprint to reach a loose ball.		 Accel = (Final Vel - Initial Vel) / Time
Unit: m/s²

📝 Topic Summary

Linear motion is all about straight or curved movement where every part of the body moves identically. It requires a direct force through the centre of mass. Remember to distinguish carefully between scalars (Distance, Speed) and vectors (Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration) as this dictates which equations you use!

💡 Examiner's Tip

In the exam, when you are asked to calculate velocity or acceleration, always show your working out and never forget the correct units (e.g., m/s or m/s²). If you forget the unit, you will lose the final mark even if the math is perfect! Also, always link your definitions to a highly specific sporting example (e.g., "Usain Bolt running the 100m straight down the track" rather than just "running").
